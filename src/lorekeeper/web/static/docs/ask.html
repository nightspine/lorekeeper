Ask about a book

Type a question in the Reader's Ask tab, such as "What happened to Aaron in the second book?". You get a short answer written from the index, up to where you are, with the entries it used listed under it.

It is for the moment when you put a series down for a month and come back unsure who someone is. The answer comes from the index, not from the book, so it is quick and cheap, and it cannot know more than your position allows.

Use it

Open a book in the Reader and choose Ask. The tab is there when the server has a model for it (see Which model answers). Type a question and press Enter. Shift+Enter starts a new line. A question can be up to 500 characters.

  • Name people as the book does. "What happened to Kiara in the second book?" works, and so does "Quincey Morris" when the index lists him as "Quincey P. Morris".
  • Say which book you mean in a series: "the second book", "book 2" or its title.
  • Follow up. "And what about his sister?" uses the entries the last answer was about.
  • Under an entry's name, Ask about types a first question for you without sending it. The command palette has Ask about this book.

Each answer lists its sources under From. A source is an entry, a detail, a connection, an event or a chapter recap. Click one to open it in the Reader. A source the index no longer shows is plain text.

What the answer can and cannot say

  • Only what you have read. The question is answered from the view at your position, the same view the other tabs show. In a series, the books you have marked finished count. Nothing later is sent to the model.
  • Only what the index says. If the index has nothing, the answer is "The index doesn't say, up to where you are." The model is told not to use what it remembers about the book, and the app drops an answer that names someone the index does not mention.
  • People you have not met yet. Ask about a name the index does not show yet and the app says so without calling a model. If you name one you know and one you do not, the answer leaves the second out and ends with a line saying the index has nothing on them.
  • Only as good as the index. A thin or wrong entry gives a thin or wrong answer. Long-lived detail is in the profile and details; a small moment the model skipped when building is not there. For a very long series, the answer may use only part of an entry's history. It says so when it cuts ("17 more connections not shown") and a narrower question brings the rest.

If you move the ruler back, or untick a Finished box, the conversation is cleared with a note, because earlier answers may mention things you are no longer meant to know. Moving forward keeps it.

What is sent where

The model gets your question, up to your last few questions, and a selection of index text: the entries the question is about, their profiles, details, connections and events, the chapter recaps near the topic, and the story so far. It never gets the book's text, and not the author. A series book's title is included so that "the second book" can be matched. The notes are capped at about 40,000 characters for a cloud model and 9,000 for Ollama.

With a cloud model, that text goes to the provider whose key you saved. The Reader says so under each answer. With Ollama, nothing leaves your computer.

Which model answers

The same small model that tidies names after a build: the cheap model of the first cloud provider with a key (Claude first), else Ollama with the default model. If neither is available the Ask tab is hidden. Opened by its address, it says to add a key under Settings. Ollama is not used while it is busy with a build.

No setting changed for this feature.

What it costs

One question sends about 9,000 to 19,000 tokens. On Claude Haiku that is 0.1 to 0.2 cents, so a hundred questions cost about a dollar at most. A local model costs nothing but time. Some questions that name nobody make one extra small call to choose entries, and a question may be retried once if the first answer breaks a rule. At most two questions are answered at the same time per server.

What is saved

Nothing. The conversation lives in the page and is gone when you reload or leave. The server keeps no questions, answers or logs of them.

For developers

The Ask routes belong to the web app, not to the /v0 protocol, and they need a session when sign-in is on. GET /api/ask says whether a model is available. POST /api/library/{key}/ask takes question, the reader's anchor and optional finished, order and history, and returns answer, found and cites. It builds the view with the gating rule, so nothing past the anchor reaches the model. Errors carry a code: bad-question, bad-position, no-model, ollama-busy, busy or model-failed. A reader app that wants the same feature can fetch the gated view over /v0 and ask its own model.
