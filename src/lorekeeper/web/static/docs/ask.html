Ask about a book

Type a question in the Reader's Ask tab, such as "What happened to Aaron in the second book?". You get a short answer written from the index, from the book's own text, or from both, up to where you are. Most answers list the sources they were written from.

It is for the moment when you put a series down for a month and come back unsure who someone is, or want to know what someone saw or said in a scene. Only index items revealed at your position, and only the part of the book before it, are sent to the model.

Use it

Open a book in the Reader and choose Ask. The tab is there when a model is set up for it (see Which model answers). Type a question and press Enter. Shift+Enter starts a new line. A question can be up to 500 characters.

  • Name people as the book does. "What happened to Kiara in the second book?" works, and so does "Quincey Morris" when the index lists him as "Quincey P. Morris".
  • In a series, say which book you mean: "the second book", "book 2" or its title.
  • Follow up. "And what about his sister?" uses the entries the last answer was about.
  • Under an entry's name, Ask about types a first question for you without sending it. The command palette has Ask about this book.
  • Ask about a chapter by number, roman numeral or name, or ask about the last or previous chapter. The chapter is looked for in the book your question names, else the book you are reading. If that book has no chapter recaps in your view, the answer says so. A chapter you have not reached has no recap in your view.

Most answers list what they were written from under From: entries, details, connections, events, chapter recaps or passages of the book. Click one to open it in the Reader. A source the index no longer shows is plain text, and so is a "story so far" source once you have read on and the recap has been replaced. An answer can come without sources, and an answer that says nothing was found has none.

Index, book or both

When the book file is on this server, a choice sits at the top of the Ask tab:

  • Index and book, the default. The answer uses the index for who is who and the order of events, and passages of the book for detail: what someone saw, said or did, and why.
  • Index only. The answer comes from the index alone, as it did before the book could be read. It is the quickest and cheapest.
  • Book only. The answer comes from passages of the book alone. It is sometimes the sharpest and sometimes finds nothing, because it has no index to fall back on.

Your choice is remembered in this browser. Without the book file, or in a community preview, there is no choice and the answer comes from the index. One quiet line under the choice says where the passages go: "Passages of the book are sent to Anthropic." (or the provider answering), or "Passages of the book are read by the local model." with Ollama. It is not shown for Index only.

Passages are found by their words, the way a search engine finds pages. A question shares words with the scene it asks about, and the names of the people it names help. A scene the book tells in quite different words can be missed, and then the answer says it was not found or falls back on the index. Asking again with words the book might use can help.

Passage sources

A passage under From is named by its chapter, such as "Chapter VI". A passage from an earlier book of a series starts with that book's short title and is plain text. Clicking a passage of the book you are reading opens it in a sheet with only the cited paragraphs, so your position stays where it is.

The sheet has Read from here. It moves your position back to the start of that passage and opens the Read tab. Because the position moves back, your questions are cleared, and the sheet says so before you press it.

What the answer can and cannot say

  • Only what you have read is sent. The question is answered from the view at your position, the same view the other tabs show. In a series, the books you have marked finished count. Nothing later is sent to the model.
  • Only the book before your position is searched. The paragraph you are on does not count, only the paragraphs before it. In a series, an earlier book's text is searched only when you have marked that book finished and it comes earlier in the reading order, and only when its book file is on this server. A later or unfinished book's text is never searched.
  • Only what the index and the book say is the aim. If they have nothing, the model is asked to say so in its own words. When the app itself refuses, the answer is "The index doesn't say, up to where you are.", "The book doesn't say, up to where you are." or "Neither the index nor the book says, up to where you are.", by the source you chose. The model is told not to use what it remembers about the book.
  • Short quotes only. The model may quote the book, but at most 8 words in a row, in quote marks. An answer that copies 9 or more words in a row gets one retry in its own words, and is refused if it still copies.
  • A check on names. Before an answer is shown, the app checks the capitalised words in it against the index text and passages it sent. A name that is not there is treated as the model's memory: the model gets one retry, and then the answer is replaced by the fixed sentence above. Your own question's words do not count as evidence. A few ordinary words are excused, such as "Chapter", the days and the months, and ordinary words that open a sentence ("However"). In a trial of 35 live questions on Claude Haiku, none was refused by this check.
  • Names you have not met. Words in your question that neither the index nor the book before your position uses are kept out of the answer. With the book as a source, a person the book has named but the index lacks counts as met. If your question names only a person you have not met, and nothing carries over from your previous question, the app says so without calling a model. If you name one person you know and one you do not, the answer is about the one you know and ends with a sentence such as "The index has nothing on Moriarty up to where you are."
  • Known gaps. The checks look at words, not at facts. A model that knows the book could still state something from memory using only names you have met, and nothing catches that. The name check also errs the other way sometimes: a sentence opening with a word that is also a name, such as "King" or "Hunter", can be refused although it is innocent.
  • Only as good as the index. With Index only, a thin or wrong entry gives a thin or wrong answer. For a long series the answer may use only part of an entry's history: the connections and events sent are limited, with the earliest and latest events favoured, and the notes say when something was left out.

Earlier answers are never sent back to the model. A follow-up carries your earlier questions, with words you have not met blanked out, and the names of the entries those answers were about, checked against the view you have now. The passages an earlier answer cited are never sent again. A turn that was refused for an unknown name or for outside knowledge is left out of the history, though it stays on screen.

When the conversation clears

The conversation and anything typed in the field, sent or not, are cleared, with the line "Cleared, because your position moved back.", when:

  • the position moves back at all, even by a paragraph and even if nothing visible changed, since fewer paragraphs of the book are then before you;
  • a Finished box is unticked, or you go back to an earlier book, change the reading order or open another index;
  • an edit or merge removes or renames something that was visible when an answer was written.

Reading on keeps it, including reading on into the next book of a series. The "story so far" recap is replaced as you read and does not clear anything. An answer that arrives after you moved back is discarded.

What is sent where

The model gets your question, your last few questions, and a selection of index text: the entries the question is about, their profiles, details, connections and events, chapter recaps, and the story so far. With the book as a source it also gets passages of the book before your position, each a paragraph and its neighbours in the same chapter, up to about 250 words. In Index and book, the passages take up to half of the amount and the index text the rest; in Book only, the index text is not sent. It never gets the author. When your view spans more than one book, the books' titles are included so that "the second book" can be matched. The amount is capped, with a lower cap for Ollama.

With a cloud model, that text, passages of the book included, goes to the first configured cloud provider (a key saved in Settings, a key from the environment, or a custom server), Claude first. One line under the question field names the model answering now. It is not kept per answer. With Ollama, nothing leaves your computer.

When the model first chooses entries

With a cloud model, some questions make one extra small call before the answer. It sends a list of entries, one line each with the name, the type and the start of its description, and gets back a few ids. It is a list of what the index shows at your position, never the book.

  • A question that names nobody, such as "Which vampire refuses to bow to anybody?". The list is the entries in your view, shortened when the view is large.
  • A "who", "whom", "whose", "which", "where", "called" or "name" question about someone named (or carried over from your last question) that has other words that matter, such as "Which horse does Gabriel ride?". The list is only the entries tied to that person or sharing an event with them, so it is not made for a person with neither. "Who is Phoebe?" sends no such list.

These lists are not sent with Ollama. Ollama still makes a small pick over names only when a question names nobody and none of its words match an entry.

Which model answers

The same choice of model the duplicate check uses: the cheap model of the first configured cloud provider (Claude first), else Ollama with the default model. The Ask tab is hidden only when no model is set up. While the local model is busy with a build, the tab stays and says so. No setting changed for this feature.

The server limits each question to 120 seconds on Ollama and on the OpenAI-compatible providers. Claude's client keeps its own default limit, which is longer.

What it costs

These are measurements on Claude Haiku, not a general figure. On one large series, about 700 entries and 385 events, a question from the index that names someone sent roughly 9,000 to 19,000 tokens, which is 0.1 to 0.2 cents. A question that names nobody, where the model first picks from the list, cost about 0.6 cents. A small or early index sends far less.

Reading the book costs a little more. On twenty questions halfway through Dracula and another long novel, a question cost about 0.1 cents with Index only, 0.15 to 0.2 cents with Index and book, and 0.1 to 0.2 cents with Book only. A question with a retry costs about twice as much. A local model costs nothing but time. A question may be retried once if the first answer breaks a rule. The server answers a couple of questions at a time and says "busy" to more.

What is saved

Nothing. The conversation lives in the page and is gone when you reload or leave. The server keeps no questions, answers or logs of them.

For developers

The Ask routes belong to the web app, not to the /v0 protocol, and they need a session when sign-in is on. GET /api/ask says whether a model is available. POST /api/library/{key}/ask takes question, the reader's anchor and optional finished, order, book, history, source and p, and returns answer, found (false when the index does not say), cites (the entries, details, connections, events and recaps it used, each with an id and a label, and the passages, each as kind "passage", an id p:<book>:<seq>, book, the first and last paragraph seq and end, chapter and number), note (why an answer was refused or changed, or null), provider, model, local (true for Ollama) and usage (calls, tokens and cost, when the provider reports them), plus source when it is not index. source is index (the default when it is absent), both or book. p is the reader's position as a fraction of the words of the book being read, from 0 to 1, and is required when source is not index; only paragraphs before the one holding that word are searched. Without the index's book file on the server, both and book answer 409 no-book-file. book is the series book the reader is showing; if the index is a different book of its series the route answers 422 bad-position. history is the last six turns as question, answer and cites; the server uses the questions and the cite ids, not the answers, and drops passage cites. The route builds the view with the gating rule, so nothing past the anchor reaches the model. Errors from the route carry a code: not-found, bad-question, bad-position, bad-source, no-book-file, no-model, ollama-busy, busy or model-failed. Sign-in (401), a missing X-Lorekeeper header (403) and malformed JSON are answered as the rest of /api does. A reader app that wants the same feature can fetch the gated view over /v0 and ask its own model.
