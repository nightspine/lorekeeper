Ask about a book

Type a question in the Reader's Ask tab, such as "What happened to Aaron in the second book?". You get a short answer written from the index, up to where you are, with, usually, the sources it was written from listed under it.

It is for the moment when you put a series down for a month and come back unsure who someone is. The answer comes from the index, not from the book, so it is quick and cheap. Only index items revealed at your position are sent to the model.

Use it

Open a book in the Reader and choose Ask. The tab is there when the server has a model for it (see Which model answers). Type a question and press Enter. Shift+Enter starts a new line. A question can be up to 500 characters.

  • Name people as the book does. "What happened to Kiara in the second book?" works, and so does "Quincey Morris" when the index lists him as "Quincey P. Morris".
  • Say which book you mean in a series: "the second book", "book 2" or its title.
  • Follow up. "And what about his sister?" uses the entries the last answer was about.
  • Under an entry's name, Ask about types a first question for you without sending it. The command palette has Ask about this book.

Most answers list what they were written from under From: entries, details, connections, events or chapter recaps. Click one to open it in the Reader. A source the index no longer shows is plain text. An answer can come without sources, when the model gave none after one retry, and an answer that says the index does not say has none.

What the answer can and cannot say

  • Only what you have read is sent. The question is answered from the view at your position, the same view the other tabs show. In a series, the books you have marked finished count. Nothing later is sent to the model.
  • Only what the index says is the aim. If the index has nothing, the model is asked to say so in its own words. When the app itself refuses, the answer is the fixed sentence "The index doesn't say, up to where you are." The model is told not to use what it remembers about the book.
  • The vocabulary check. Before an answer is shown, every capitalised word of two letters or more must be a word the model was sent in the notes. Your own question's words do not count. Fixed exceptions are "I", Book, Chapter, Part, Volume, the days and the months. At the start of a sentence an ordinary English word from a built-in list is also excused ("However", "Meanwhile"), unless it is also a common name or title such as Hunter, Baker, King or Page. A hyphenated name passes only if the whole word or every part of it is in the notes. A name that fails gets one retry, and then the answer is replaced by the fixed sentence. In 35 live questions on Claude Haiku, none was refused by this check and all ten questions that name nobody were found.
  • Words and names you have not met. Every word of your question that is in no part of your view and is not an ordinary word is banned from the answer. A capitalised one of two letters or more (so "Li" and "Jo" count) that does not start a sentence, or that follows a title such as Dr. or Mr., is treated as a name. A question whose only name is such an unknown one is refused without a model call, but only when the question names no entry and nothing carries over from your previous question. "Does Moriarty eat flies?" is refused even if "flies" matches another entry, because no entry is named. After a question about Renfield, "Does Moriarty return?" does reach the model: the name is banned from the answer and the fixed sentence is appended. A lowercase or sentence-first unknown word ("Moriarty?", "who is moriarty?") is banned from the answer but does not cause the refusal, since it may be an ordinary rare word. If you name one entry you know and one you do not, the answer is about the one you know, leaves the other out, and ends with a sentence such as "The index has nothing on Moriarty up to where you are."
  • The known gap. The checks look at words, not at facts. A model that knows the book could still state something from memory using only names you have met, and nothing catches that. A made-up name that is an ordinary English word is caught in the middle of a sentence, but not when it opens a sentence and is not a name-like word. The reverse also happens: a sentence that opens with a name-like common word, such as "Hunter" or "King", is refused even when it is innocent, unless the notes hold the word.
  • Only as good as the index. A thin or wrong entry gives a thin or wrong answer. Long-lived detail is in the profile and details; a small moment the model skipped when building is not there. For a long series, the answer may use only part of an entry's history. At most 12 connections are sent per entry (current ones first), and when an entry has many events a sample is sent, with the first and the last six put first in line. They are not guaranteed: with several entries selected or a small limit, some can still be left out. The notes say when something was cut ("17 more connections not shown").

Earlier answers are never sent back to the model. A follow-up carries your earlier questions and the names of the entries those answers were about, checked against the view you have now. In the earlier questions, every word that is unknown to your current view is replaced by "…", so a name you had not met cannot be passed along. A turn that was refused for an unknown name or for outside knowledge is left out of the history altogether, though it stays on screen.

Questions about a chapter

"What happened in chapter 12?", "chapter XII", "the last chapter" and "the previous chapter" are matched against the chapter recaps by title. Arabic and roman numbers count as equal. The chapter is looked for in the book your question names, else the book you are reading. "Last", "latest", "this" and "current" mean the newest chapter recap of the current book, and "previous" the one before it. The parts of a long chapter are sent whole when together they fit in half the size limit. Otherwise each part is shortened evenly. When even that cannot fit, the first and last parts and a spread of those between are sent, with a line saying how many are not shown. They are always sent ahead of other recaps. A chapter title written out in the question also matches. If the book has no chapter recaps in your view, the answer is told so and does not borrow another book's. A chapter you have not reached has no recap in your view, so it cannot be asked about.

When the conversation clears

The thread and anything typed in the field are cleared, with the line "Cleared, because your position moved back.", when the view shows less than it did when an answer was written. The rule is about the whole view at that time, not only the items the answer used: anything that was visible then and is not now clears it. The one exception is the "story so far" recap, which is replaced as you read on and never clears the thread. That happens when you:

  • move to an earlier position in the same book, even by one paragraph that changes what is shown;
  • untick a Finished box, or switch to an earlier book or another reading order;
  • open another index;
  • edit or merge so that something visible then is gone or has a different name.

Moving forward keeps the thread, and so does a step that leaves the view as it was. The line appears only when something was cleared. An answer that arrives after you moved back is discarded.

What is sent where

The model gets your question, your last few questions, and a selection of index text: the entries the question is about, their profiles, details, connections and events, the chapter recaps near the topic, and the story so far. It never gets the book's text, and not the author. A series book's title is included so that "the second book" can be matched. The notes are capped at about 40,000 characters for a cloud model and 9,000 for Ollama.

The server gives each question 120 seconds on Ollama and on the OpenAI-compatible providers. Claude's client keeps its own default limit, which is longer.

With a cloud model, that text goes to the first configured cloud provider (a key saved in Settings, a key from the environment, or a custom server), Claude first. One line under the question field names the model answering now. It is not kept per answer, so it can change if the model available to the server changes. With Ollama, nothing leaves your computer.

When the model first chooses entries

With a cloud model, two kinds of question make one extra small call before the answer. The extra call sends a list of entries, one line each with the name, the type and the first 140 characters or so of its description, and gets back at most five ids. It is a list of what the index shows at your position, never the book.

  • A question that names nobody, such as "Which vampire refuses to bow to anybody?". The list is every entry in your view, up to about 120,000 characters. Over that, descriptions are shortened and then the entries with the least written about them are left out.
  • A "who", "whom", "whose", "which", "where" or "called" question about someone named or carried over from your last question, when the question has other words that matter, such as "Which horse does Gabriel ride?". The list is only the entries tied to that person or sharing an event with them, up to about 30,000 characters. "Who is Phoebe?" has no other words that matter and sends no such list.

These two lists are not sent with Ollama, whose context is too small for them. Ollama still makes a small pick over names only when a question names nobody and none of its words match an entry. Otherwise a question that names nobody is matched against the words of the entries.

Which model answers

The same choice of model the duplicate check uses: the cheap model of the first configured cloud provider (Claude first), else Ollama with the default model. If neither is available the Ask tab is hidden. Opened by its address, it says to add a key under Settings. Ollama is not used while it is busy with a build.

No setting changed for this feature.

What it costs

These are measurements on one large series, about 700 entries and 385 events, not a general figure. A small or early index sends far less. There, a question that names someone sent about 9,000 to 19,000 tokens, which on Claude Haiku is 0.1 to 0.2 cents. A question that names nobody, where the model first picks from the list of entries, cost about 0.6 cents because the list is sent too. A "who" or "which" question about a named person adds a smaller list, roughly 4,000 to 7,000 tokens. A local model costs nothing but time. A question is retried once at most if the first answer breaks a rule, and the answer says nothing about it. At most two questions run at the same time per server, and only a question that is running holds one of those places. A question you cancel before it starts takes none.

What is saved

Nothing. The conversation lives in the page and is gone when you reload or leave. The server keeps no questions, answers or logs of them.

For developers

The Ask routes belong to the web app, not to the /v0 protocol, and they need a session when sign-in is on. GET /api/ask says whether a model is available. POST /api/library/{key}/ask takes question, the reader's anchor and optional finished, order, book and history, and returns answer, found (false when the index does not say), cites (the entries, details, connections, events and recaps it used, each with an id and a label), note (why an answer was refused or changed, or null), provider, model, local (true for Ollama) and usage (calls, tokens and cost, when the provider reports them). book is the series book the reader is showing; if the index is a different book of its series the route answers 422 bad-position. history is the last six turns as question, answer and cites; the server uses the questions and the cite ids, not the answers. It builds the view with the gating rule, so nothing past the anchor reaches the model. Errors from the route carry a code: not-found, bad-question, bad-position, no-model, ollama-busy, busy or model-failed. Sign-in (401), a missing X-Lorekeeper header (403) and malformed JSON are answered as the rest of /api does. A reader app that wants the same feature can fetch the gated view over /v0 and ask its own model.
